# 🎬 LTX-2.5 Studio

A Gradio studio for [LTX-2.5](https://huggingface.co/Lightricks/LTX-2.5) — Lightricks'
22B joint **video + audio** generation model — covering every mode the model supports,
with **user-uploadable LoRA at inference time**.

Built on `diffusers`, pinned to an exact commit. Deploys to a public Hugging Face Space on
ZeroGPU: [`techfreakworm/LTX2.5-Studio`](https://huggingface.co/spaces/techfreakworm/LTX2.5-Studio).
It also runs on an Apple-silicon Mac with enough memory ([below](#run-it-locally-apple-silicon)).

> **Where things stand, 2026-11-16.** The Space runs the build deployed that day, deploy 1,
> with that day's fixes. On it all eleven modes ran, with Upscale ×2, both decoders, an adapter
> from the Hub, prompt enhancement and the model-chosen length, and every result was looked at
> and measured; on a Mac all eleven modes are verified on real weights too. The record, run by
> run: [`docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md`](docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md) §10.1 (the Space) and §10.2
> (the Mac); what deploy 1 contains, and what was open at it:
> [`docs/RELEASE_NOTES.md`](docs/RELEASE_NOTES.md).

---

## The one thing to understand first

**LTX-2.5 is jointly audio-visual and there is no video-off switch.**

The pipeline returns `LTX2PipelineOutput(frames=..., audio=...)` — both non-optional
tensors. Every call spends full video compute and produces a video, whichever track you
asked for. There is no flag that turns the video half off.

So the three modes that return only sound still render a video and throw it away, and you
pay for that GPU time. The app says so on screen where you pick one of them, and applies the
mitigations it can: it renders at 256 × 256, never runs Upscale ×2, and exports only the
sound. The other way round, the three silent-video modes render the sound and drop it, at the
same GPU time as with sound.

---

## The 11 modes

Eleven modes, but **not eleven code paths**: a presentation layer over two upstream
workflows (`text2video` and `condition`) plus the audio-latent input route and single-track
export. The UI is generated from the registry in `ltx25_studio/modes.py`, so the modes below
and the widgets on screen cannot drift apart.

| # | Mode | Needs | Can also take | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Text to video | text | — | video |
| 2 | Image to video | image | — | video |
| 3 | Image + text to video | image, text | — | video |
| 4 | Video to video | video | text | video: a variation of your clip |
| 5 | Text to video with sound | text | — | video with sound, and its sound as a `.wav` |
| 6 | Image to video with sound | image | — | video with sound, and its sound as a `.wav` |
| 7 | Image + text to video with sound | image, text | — | video with sound, and its sound as a `.wav` |
| 8 | Text to audio | text | — | sound (`.wav`) ⚠️ still renders video internally |
| 9 | Video to audio | video | text | sound (`.wav`) ⚠️ same |
| 10 | Audio to audio | audio | text | sound (`.wav`) ⚠️ same |
| 11 | Audio to video | audio | text, image (as the first frame) | video carrying **your** audio, and that audio as a `.wav`; pays the audio encode on top |

A run that is missing a required input is refused before a GPU is reserved, and an input a
mode does not take is dropped with a note rather than used.

Sound the model makes over full scale is turned down as a whole, to peak at −0.1 dBFS, instead of
being clipped, in the video's sound track and the `.wav` alike, and the run's diagnostics say by
how many dB. Your own recording, carried into the file by audio to video, is never changed.

**Video to video** makes a variation of your clip, steered by the prompt if you give one. The
clip is noised to one of three of the model's own levels and denoised from there; **How much to
change your clip** picks the level: *A little* (0.42), *Moderately* (0.73) or *A lot* (0.91, the
default). Verified on real weights on a Mac: the result's similarity to the clip (SSIM) was
0.78, 0.65 and 0.36–0.40 at the three levels, and the prompt visibly changes the picture only
at *A lot*; at the lower two the clip comes back re-rendered with small changes. It runs without
a prompt too. The length is always the clip's. A phone clip stored sideways comes out upright,
and a 60 fps clip is read in real time.

**Audio is not a first-class argument** — there is no `audio=` kwarg anywhere in the API. Your
audio is encoded to `audio_latents`, and then:

- **Audio to video** holds your audio fixed while the picture is generated to it. The file's
  sound track is your recording itself, cut to the video's length. By default the video takes
  its length from the recording (the longest length that fits in it); a set length longer than
  the recording is refused before anything runs. The prompt is optional, and so is an image for
  the first frame.
  With a speech recording and no first-frame picture, some seeds come out as a black-and-white period film: add “in colour” to the prompt, or give a first-frame picture.
  (On the Space the same recording and prompt came out black and white at one seed and in
  colour at another, and in colour with a first-frame picture.)
- **Audio to audio** changes your audio by one of the model's own levels: 0.42, 0.73 or 0.91
  (*A little*, *Moderately*, *A lot*), or 1.0 (*Ignore my audio (new sound)*), which makes
  fresh sound.
- With automatic length on, these two modes take their length from your audio file: the
  duration predictor reads only the text, so it cannot hear the upload.

Where a mode offers **Let the model pick the length**, the model's duration predictor chooses it
within the range you allow, and the run's note says what it chose. When its own figure lies
outside the range, the note says so and what the range made of it: "The model chose 4.5 s; your
shortest length is 5 s, so the video is 5.0 s."

What the modes that take or make sound were measured to do, on real weights on a Mac, after the
audio-encoding fix of 2026-11-16 (until then every uploaded sound reached the model about 9 dB
too quiet and duller, because the app's mel transform used the wrong window size):

- **Audio to audio** returns sound at your input's own level at *A little* and *Moderately*
  (within 1.5 dB), changed as much as you chose: on speech, the same sound events at the same
  times scored 0.97, 0.95 and 0.71 at the three levels, and the words came back exactly at
  *A little*.
- **Video to audio** makes sound for what is visible. From a silent clip of a man shouting it
  made a voice in time with his mouth (its loudness follows the clip's original sound track at
  a correlation of 0.92), in words of its own.
- **Audio to video** carries your recording exactly and takes its length from it, with or
  without a prompt and with or without a first-frame image. It does not lip-sync (next
  paragraph).

**Speech and lips.** Audio to video does not lip-sync. Measured on real weights, the mouth's
opening frame by frame against the speech level of the sound track: in audio to video it did
not follow the words in any run (correlation between −0.38 and +0.07, no better than an
unrelated sound track scores), while speech the model writes itself, in image + text to video
with sound, scores +0.84. Asking in the prompt for perfect lip sync and quoting the words did not
change that in any case tried. Two changes to how the app samples — re-noising your audio along
with the picture, plus cross-modal guidance — reached at most +0.26 in a probe, and neither is in
the app. Lightricks' own audio-driven lip-sync recipes for LTX-2.3 use the non-distilled model;
this app runs the distilled LTX-2.5 transformer, unguided. So, for a face that speaks in time,
use **Image + text to video with sound** and write the words in quotes in your prompt; the
model voices them itself. Audio to video sets a picture to a recording — its mood, its
content, its length — and does not lip-sync. The measurements are in
[`docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md`](docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md) §10.2.

Through the API, pass `noise_scale` yourself for video to video and audio to audio: the
endpoint's published default, 0.5, is snapped to the nearest level, 0.42 (*A little*), in both.
The page always sends the level you chose. The status Markdown an API call gets back carries the
run's diagnostics, which name each attached adapter ("adapter bts at 0.8: attached to 1632
layers") and give the run's time split into the progress bar's own phases ("took 23.4 s on cuda:
loading the model 1.2 s, preparing the inputs 0.3 s, sampling 14.0 s, decoding the frames 4.3 s,
writing the file 1.1 s").

## LoRA — the headline feature

Bring your own adapter, by **Hub repo id** (`org/name`) or **direct upload** of a
`.safetensors` file. Each adapter you stage has its own strength; adapters are often made for
0.5 to 1.2.

The reason this gets so much care: upstream's *only* validation is a substring test that
every LoRA key contains `lora`. An adapter built for a completely different architecture
passes that test, attaches to **zero** modules, and then generation proceeds normally and
produces output that looks like the base model — with no error raised anywhere. Silent
garbage.

So every adapter goes through a staged validator, and the UI tells you exactly which stage
rejected one and why, quoting the validator's own message rather than flattening it to
"invalid file":

| stage | check |
|---|---|
| 1 | size bound — refused before any download |
| 2 | header-only parse — a multi-GB file is **never** materialised just to validate it |
| 3 | format — safetensors with a parseable header (not-safetensors and bad-header are different messages) |
| 4 | version — reads `model_version`, `ss_ltx_version`, `modelspec.architecture`, `ss_base_model_version` in priority order; an LTX-**2.3** adapter is warned about, not refused |
| 5 | key convention — on unknown, the error **names the prefixes actually found** |
| 5.5 | in-context adapters — an IC-LoRA on the plain pipeline neither errors nor works |
| 6 | pairing — the two low-rank halves of every layer must pair |
| 6.5 | shape fit and target modules — wrong dimension and wrong module are different diagnoses. Checked when you validate, against the transformer's layer shapes shipped with the app, with no weights loaded |
| 7 | **attach assertion** — after loading, assert the adapter attached to more than zero modules |

Stage 7 is the one upstream does not do, and it runs on every load. For a Hub repo the file
is downloaded before stages 2–6.5 run (its size is checked first), so a wrongly-built adapter
costs one bounded download, kept in a size-limited cache.

The panel shows the detected key convention, the rank (read from the tensor shapes), and the
alpha and `model_version` when the file declares them. **"Not declared" is the normal,
expected reading** — of three real public LTX LoRAs measured for this design, none carried
`model_version` and two had no metadata block at all. Absence warns; it does not reject,
because rejecting on absence would reject the most downloaded LTX adapter on the Hub.

On real weights (locally, on a Mac) two public LTX-2.5 adapters visibly change the output
when given their trigger words. On the Space one of them, at 1.0 with its trigger word, passed
Validate and gave a different picture from the same prompt and seed without it. An
adapter that needs trigger words cannot show that effect in the two modes that take no prompt
(image to video, image to video with sound).

## Limits the app checks before it reserves a GPU

Two hard model constraints, and upstream handles them very differently:

**`width % 32 == 0` and `height % 32 == 0`** — upstream raises. You lose a run, but you
find out.

**`num_frames % 8 == 1`** — upstream does **not** raise. It computes
`(num_frames − 1) // 8 + 1` and **silently floors**: ask for 120 frames and you get 113.

This app refuses an illegal size or frame count before any GPU time is reserved. The hint
beside the fields names the nearest legal values, and **Snap to legal values** sets them when
you press it; nothing you typed is rewritten behind your back. The `8` is
`vae.temporal_compression_ratio`, used as a documented pre-load default so the UI can validate
before any VAE exists, with a load-time tripwire that raises if upstream ever changes it.

The app's own limits, not the model's: at most 1920 pixels a side, 481 frames, 20 seconds.

On the Space there is one more, the size and length it has been measured to finish. A
request whose first or Upscale pass would hold more than 64,000 latent tokens
((width / 32) × (height / 32) × ((frames − 1) / 8 + 1)), or whose returned video would pass
520 million pixel-frames, is refused before any GPU time, with a sentence that says what fits:
"This size and length is more than this Space has been measured to finish. At 1888 × 1120,
up to 9.7 s fits. Make it smaller or shorter." The largest run measured to finish returned
1920 × 1088 at 10 s, through Upscale ×2 from 960 × 544 (63,240 tokens in its Upscale pass,
503,439,360 pixel-frames). Off the Space this limit does not apply.

### Prompt enhancement

**Off by default.** Ticking *Enhance my prompt* rewrites your line into a full LTX-2.5
caption with the model's own system prompt — the image-conditioned one when you supply an
image — and then generates from the rewrite. The prompt actually sent is printed in the run
report, because an enhancer that silently swaps your prompt is worse than none.

**What it costs.** The enhancer is `google/gemma-4-E2B-it-qat-mobile-transformers` (2.49 GB,
public, ungated, pinned to one revision). It runs on the GPU inside the run's own
reservation, so an enhanced run **reserves 80 s more**. The rewrite itself takes a few
seconds; the enhancer then stays loaded for the life of the GPU worker, and the first enhanced
run in a fresh worker also pays about 29 s to load it. Point `LTX25_PROMPT_ENHANCER_REPO` at
`google/gemma-4-E2B-it` for the 10.28 GB bf16 build instead (that repo loads unpinned).

Why it is placed that way, as measured: on the CPU the rewrite took about nine minutes; freed
after every rewrite it cost +30.78 s a run (46.53 s against 15.75 s at 256×256×9); kept
loaded on the full `xlarge` GPU, it fits: on the Space after the 2026-11-16 fixes an enhanced
run peaked at 61.43 GiB reserved of 94.97 GiB, and the largest peak of that day's 24 runs,
63.30 GiB, still left more than 31 GiB spare.

On the Space the enhancer's weights come with the model's: `preload_from_hub` fetches its 8
files (2.49 GB) before the app starts, so the first enhanced run does not pay the ~88 s
download inside its reservation. On a CUDA machine start-up also fetches them into the Hub
cache in the background, which on the Space finds them already there
(`LTX25_PREFETCH_ENHANCER=0` declines the ~2.5 GB). Before the preload, that start-up fetch
was all there was: it failed on the Space's build of 2026-08-26 (seen in the log of
2026-11-13, for a reason that applies to every boot) and ran on deploy 1. The pinned
transformers (5.23.0) refuses one
per-layer scale table of the enhancer's checkpoint, so the app reads that one weight back from
the cached checkpoint and refuses any other mismatch. With that, both the text and the image
route rewrite prompts on real weights on a Mac, and the text route does on the Space
(2026-11-16).

There is deliberately **no seed control** for it. LTX-2.5 enhancement decodes greedily, so
`prompt_enhancement_seed` changes nothing — an advertised lever attached to nothing is a
defect this project has already found seven of.

### Several shots in one clip

**There is no multishot or scene API in LTX-2.5.** To ask for several shots, write them one
after another in the prompt. The UI does not offer a shot list: the one it had fed prompt text
to the model as if it were frames and never worked. Leave prompt enhancement **off** for such
a prompt: the 2.5 enhancer's system prompts tell the model *not* to describe scene cuts, so it
would flatten your shots into one continuous take. Prompt-structured shots are not verified.

## Run the tests

```bash
python3.12 -m venv .venv                       # 3.12 is required, not preferred
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
.venv/bin/python -m pip install -e ".[dev]"    # pytest + ruff
.venv/bin/python -m pytest -q
PYTHON=.venv/bin/python scripts/gate.sh
```

No GPU, no weights, no network. A venv without torch passes too, but it skips the checks that
compare our fakes and layouts with real torch, torchaudio, diffusers and safetensors, so it
cannot vouch for them. `scripts/gate.sh` runs pytest, then ruff if it is installed, and prints
the interpreter and the torch version, so a result says which environment it came from.

`.venv/bin/python app.py` starts the UI. On a machine without an NVIDIA GPU it starts with no
generation device: Generate explains how to opt in (next section).

- **Python 3.12 is a hard requirement.** numpy 2.5.5 needs Python 3.12 or newer and `av`
  18.1.0 needs 3.11 or newer. Every requirement is pinned to one version, so an older Python
  fails the install rather than resolving older packages.
- `spaces` is deliberately **absent** from `requirements.txt`. The Spaces platform installs
  its own version and a conflicting pin breaks resolution at build time; the import is guarded
  in `app.py`, so local runs work without it.
- You need an `HF_TOKEN` with access to `Lightricks/LTX-2.5-Diffusers` to download the
  weights; the repo is gated.

## Run it locally (Apple silicon)

The app's own code can generate on an Apple-silicon Mac's GPU (MPS), with the distilled
transformer as a Q8_0 GGUF file and every other component from the pinned snapshot. It is one
code path with the Space: the same `generate()` and the same stage graph, in which the local
runner decides only what is in memory when (by default the text encoder and the transformer are
never in memory together). This is how all eleven modes were verified on 2026-11-16, on a Mac
with **128 GB** of unified memory. Smaller machines are not verified.

**What you need.**

- **A separate Python 3.12 environment** with PyTorch 2.15.0, torchvision 0.29.2 and
  torchaudio 2.11.1 in place of the trio `requirements.txt` pins, plus `gguf` (0.19.0 was
  used), and every other pin from `requirements.txt` except `bitsandbytes` and `kernels`,
  which are CUDA-only. PyTorch 2.14 or newer: 2.15.0 is what was verified on real weights, and
  2.14.1 measured the same in the numerics checks. **PyTorch 2.11, the Space's pin, computes
  attention wrongly on MPS**, and the app's own numerics self-test, run at startup, refuses to
  generate on it; `scripts/local_generate.py` runs the same self-test and exits refused before
  loading anything.
- **The weights, about 55 GB**, in the Hugging Face cache:
  - from `Lightricks/LTX-2.5-Diffusers` at revision `871165de`, everything except the
    transformer shards, the latent upsamplers and the diffusion decoder (those three are
    CUDA-only here) — text encoder, connectors, VAE, audio VAE, vocoder, tokenizer, duration
    head, scheduler and configs: 32.1 GB;
  - `distilled/ltx-2.5-22b-distilled-Q8_0.gguf` from `unsloth/LTX-2.5-GGUF` (revision
    `ac3c540c` was used): 22.8 GB.

  Nothing is downloaded for you on a Mac: a missing file is reported with the exact
  `hf download` command that fetches it.

**Settings** (environment variables; set them before you start the app):

| variable | value | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `LTX25_DEVICE` | `mps` | generate on the Mac's GPU. The default, `auto`, uses an NVIDIA GPU where there is one and otherwise starts with no generation device |
| `LTX25_TRANSFORMER_GGUF` | path to the `.gguf` file | load the transformer from it, instead of the snapshot's 38 GB of bf16 shards |
| `LTX25_RESIDENCY` | `staged` (the default on macOS) | the text encoder and the transformer are never in memory together: per run, encode the prompt, release the encoder, then load the transformer. `resident` keeps both (not verified) |
| `LTX25_MODEL_SNAPSHOT_DIR` | leave unset | unset, the app finds the pinned snapshot in the Hugging Face cache. Set, it loads the components from that directory instead, through the same staged runner; only a checkpoint under 1 GB (such as the test suite's tiny one) is loaded all at once |
| `PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO`, `PYTORCH_MPS_LOW_WATERMARK_RATIO` | optional; the runs used `0.5` and `0.4` | caps on PyTorch's MPS allocator. **If you set them, the low one must not exceed the high one**: setting only the high one below 1.4 leaves the low one at PyTorch's default of 1.4, and then every GPU operation fails. The app refuses such a pair before it loads anything: an MPS start stops on it in its own words before the numerics self-test runs, so it is never reported as a PyTorch that computes wrongly |
| `LTX25_MEMORY_LIMIT_GB` | 90 on macOS by default | before each big load and before the denoise, the app checks that committed memory plus what that step adds stays under this, and refuses with the numbers if not |
| `LTX25_MEMORY_SETTLE_SECONDS` | 2 by default, 0 to 60 | the longest that check may wait for the gauge: for memory just freed to leave it, and to read a failing check again before it refuses (below). `0` switches the wait off: one reading |
| `LTX25_MPS_SELF_TEST` | `enforce` (default) or `warn` | what to do when PyTorch fails the MPS numerics self-test |
| `LTX25_OUTPUT_DIR` | `outputs` by default | where each run's files are written. A folder outside the working directory and the system temp dir is passed to Gradio as the one allowed path, so the page can show the results written there; for that launch it takes the place of `GRADIO_ALLOWED_PATHS` |

**The memory check waits for memory just freed.** On a Mac, GPU memory the app frees leaves
the gauge over a moment, not at once: after the text encoder's release the kernel gave its 24 GB
back over 0.1–0.35 s, so a reading taken at once was 3.5–23 GB too high, and with a browser and
test suites running it refused a run that fitted. So after a release the check reads the gauge
once it has stopped falling (two readings 50 ms apart within 0.25 GB), and a check that fails is
read again every 0.1 s and passes as soon as a reading fits; both within
`LTX25_MEMORY_SETTLE_SECONDS`. On real weights the settled readings were 3 to 13 GB below the
first ones, reached in 0.16–0.36 s, and the three runs the early reading had refused ran. A run
the check refuses is reported as a memory refusal, a failure of its own kind, not as an error in
the app.

**One worked run** (the verified 704×480, 5-second text to video, through
`scripts/local_generate.py`, which drives the app's own `generate()`):

```bash
SNAP=~/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--Lightricks--LTX-2.5-Diffusers/snapshots/871165de037793df7d75c23a02dd7f45fd364c97
GGUF=~/.cache/huggingface/hub/models--unsloth--LTX-2.5-GGUF/snapshots/ac3c540c6025903784336e99b2dc179385c4c3b7/distilled/ltx-2.5-22b-distilled-Q8_0.gguf

PYTORCH_MPS_HIGH_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.5 PYTORCH_MPS_LOW_WATERMARK_RATIO=0.4 HF_HUB_OFFLINE=1 \
  .venv-mps/bin/python scripts/local_generate.py \
    --mode text_to_video --device mps \
    --prompt "A red vintage tram climbs a steep cobbled street at golden hour, slow push-in. The tram bell rings twice and steel wheels grind on the rails." \
    --width 704 --height 480 --num-frames 121 --seed 7 \
    --snapshot-dir "$SNAP" --transformer-gguf "$GGUF" --json run.json
```

`.venv-mps` is the separate environment above. The script takes its paths as flags; add
`--dry-run` to print the plan and the memory arithmetic without loading anything. To use the
UI instead, start `app.py` from that environment with `LTX25_DEVICE=mps` and
`LTX25_TRANSFORMER_GGUF="$GGUF"`. Through the UI on real weights, in a real browser
(2026-11-16): audio to video, and text to video with an adapter uploaded on the page, finished;
text to video with sound was refused by the memory check's early reading. On a build with that
check fixed (above) it then finished through the page, and the file the browser downloaded was
identical to the command-line run's. In that second session, with other heavy jobs on the
machine, audio to video was refused by the memory check (`65.5 + 27.5 = 93.0 GB` against its
90 GB limit), and the test machine's own memory watchdog stopped the session while the adapter
run loaded the transformer, so those two have not been run through the page on the fixed
build.

Started this way on a Mac, the page names where the run happens and shows no meter: its cost
panel reads "Runs on this Mac's GPU; nothing is metered" where the Space's shows what a run
reserves, needs free and is billed, and nothing on the page speaks of quota, reserving,
billing or paying.

**What it costs in memory, as measured** (the whole machine's committed memory — active,
wired and compressed pages — on the 128 GB Mac): 57–61 GB at 704×480 with 49–121 frames,
and 70 GB at 193 frames (8 s); across that day's earlier runs, at 704×480 or 480×704 with up to
193 frames, the peaks were 57–72 GB, and runs made while other heavy jobs shared the machine
read up to 75 GB. The figure is the whole machine's, so it moves with whatever else runs: the
final pass on the final code, with other jobs' test suites coming and going, peaked at
66.5–78.2 GB. Up to 121 frames the peak comes from loading the text encoder or the transformer,
not from the denoise. With Enhance on, 65.0 and 69.6 GB were measured at 704×480 with 49 frames
(the text and the image route), against 57 GB for the same size without it.

**When the model picks the length**, the memory check budgets the denoise for the longest clip
the model may return, set by the longest length you allow, not for the length it then picks.
At 704×480, up to 10 s is 233 frames, about 27.6 GB of activations, so on this Mac that setting
needs a quiet machine: with test suites running, the check refused it (`64.0 + 27.6 = 91.6 GB`).
Up to 8 s ran at a peak of 84.5 GB with a base load of about 46 GB; the model chose 5.0 s.

**How long it takes.** At 704×480×121 (5 s of video), the denoise and decode took 142 s on a
quiet machine and 333 s with other work running, plus about a minute of loading; 193 frames
(8 s) took 563 s. The progress bar names what is happening: "loading the model" while the text
encoder and the transformer load, and "decoding the frames" once the last step is done, with
either decoder (on the Space too).

**What does not run locally.** Upscale ×2, the model's diffusion decoder and bitsandbytes int8
need an NVIDIA GPU (CUDA). On a Mac the default decoder setting decodes with the conv VAE and
the run says so; asking for the diffusion decoder or for Upscale ×2 is refused before anything
runs, except at very small sizes such as the test checkpoint's.

## ZeroGPU and quota — the actual numbers

Generation runs inside a `@spaces.GPU` reservation on shared, MIG-partitioned hardware.
This is metered, and the meter is the resource you will actually run out of.

| | |
|---|---|
| Hardware | NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition (sm_120), not the A10G the id string `zero-a10g` implies |
| GPU size | **`xlarge`**, 94.971 GiB measured. The app asks for it explicitly: `LTX25_ZEROGPU_SIZE` defaults to `xlarge`. With `auto` the platform hands out `large`, 47.375 GiB measured, which is where this Space ran until 2026-08-26 ([`docs/MIG_ALLOCATOR_SIGHTINGS.md`](docs/MIG_ALLOCATOR_SIGHTINGS.md)) |
| Multiplier | **× 2 on `xlarge`** (× 1 on `large`): Hugging Face's documented rate. Billed seconds cannot be read back, so it has not been measured here |
| PRO quota | 2400 GPU-seconds a day, as Hugging Face documents it. When it resets has not been verified |
| Weights | from the image: before the app starts, `preload_from_hub` (the README's front matter) fetches the 35 model files the Space loads (71.92 GB; the temporal upsampler, which nothing loads, is no longer fetched) and the prompt enhancer's 8 files (2.49 GB). Start-up checks the cache and downloads only what is missing, saying how many files and GB, and refuses a download the 150 GB disk could not hold before writing a byte. How long a cold start takes with the preload has not been measured |

**What one run reserves** (`zerogpu.estimate_duration`) follows its size and length. It is a
fit of the Space's own measurements ([`docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md`](docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md)
§10.3): the seconds before the first step, eight denoise steps whose time grows with the latent
tokens they hold, then the decode and the write, which grow with the pixels returned; Upscale ×2
adds its three steps at twice the width and height; adapters are allowed 30 s and prompt
enhancement 60 s. Each part is reserved at 1.3 × its time, rounded up to 5 s, and the run's
own part is never below 45 s. To start, a run needs its reservation × 2 of quota free; it is
then billed for the GPU time it actually uses, × 2.

| run (text to video with sound) | reserves | quota it needs free to start |
|---|---:|---:|
| 704×480, 5 s (121 frames): the default | 60 s | 120 s |
| the same with Upscale ×2 | 105 s | 210 s |
| the same with adapters | 100 s | 200 s |
| the same with prompt enhancement | 140 s | 280 s |
| the same with all three | 225 s | 450 s |
| 1280×704, 10 s | 145 s | 290 s |
| 960×544, 10 s, Upscale ×2 (returns 1920×1088) | 240 s | 480 s |

A sound-only mode, at its 256×256, reserves 45 s for 5 s of sound. So at the default size
Upscale ×2 adds 45 s, and more for a larger or longer video; adapters add 40 s, however many;
prompt enhancement adds 80 s. `LTX25_GPU_DURATION_SECONDS`, which used to set a flat 180 s, is
ignored: if it is set, start-up says so once. `LTX25_GPU_DURATION_FLOOR_SECONDS`, unset by
default, raises every reservation to at least that many seconds, should an estimate ever
prove short.

What runs drew on the Space: on 2026-11-16, under deploy 1's flat 180 s, 24 runs in all
eleven modes had GPU windows of 10.5–51.6 s, so a plain run at 704×480 with 121 frames drew
at most 34–65 GPU-s (its window × 2) while it needed 360 s of quota free to start. Six larger
runs on 2026-11-17, up to 1920×1088 at 10 s, took 52–154 s (§10.3). The longest reservation
the platform accepts has not been measured.

Things worth knowing as a user:

- **A run that fails on the GPU still costs quota**, and so does a timeout. Most input
  problems — a missing input, an illegal size or length, audio shorter than the video you
  asked for, a file that cannot be read — are refused before a GPU is reserved and cost
  nothing.
- **A timeout is never retried automatically.** A retry would reuse the request's ZeroGPU
  proxy token (`X-IP-Token`), which has expired by then, so it would fail with "Expired
  ZeroGPU proxy token" and report the wrong cause, and spend a second reservation doing it.
  Press Generate again for a fresh request.

**Precision on the Space.** `LTX25_QUANTIZATION` defaults to `auto`: bitsandbytes int8 for
the transformer and the text encoder on ZeroGPU, where bf16 does not fit the disk cap, and
bf16 everywhere else. 316 of the transformer's 1660 linear layers — its conditioning path —
stay in bf16: quantising them is what turned every Space output into colour static until
2026-08-25. bitsandbytes' warning that int8 inputs are cast from bf16 to float16 reaches the log
once per process, not once per matrix product, so the Space's log keeps its start-up report and
its real warnings.

**The decoder.** On CUDA the default, `LTX25_DECODER=auto`, decodes with the model's own
diffusion decoder wherever it can run (its kernel available, the video large enough) and with
the conv VAE otherwise, and the run says which. The diffusion decoder is built once, at
start-up, beside the pipeline, so on the Space it is packed with the rest and every run uses
that one instance; if that build fails, start-up logs one line and each run builds its own, as
before. Before 2026-11-16 the diffusion decoder ran only with Upscale ×2. On the Space after the
fixes, every run that returned video at the default was decoded by the diffusion decoder, and
its report said so.

On the page the choice reads **Auto** (the default, as above: the vendor's own),
**Steadier (diffusion decoder)** or **Sharper (standard decoder)**. What you see: on the
Space, the same run through both gives the same scene; the diffusion decoder is a little
softer and steadier from frame to frame, and the standard decoder is sharper frame by frame
and shimmers more. The diffusion decode costs about 2.5 s more per run there (4.4 s against
1.9 s at 704×480 with 121 frames).

### Upscale ×2 (stage 2)

A latent upsample plus three more denoise steps, doubling width and height. **Off by
default**, because it is a real quota cost (45 s more reserved at the default size, more for a
larger or longer video). It needs CUDA, so it
runs only on the Space. The three sound-only modes never run it, and the app says so for them.

What is known: 704×480 with 49 frames passes on `xlarge` (55.906 GiB reserved, measured
2026-08-26; the frames of that run were not looked at). Until 2026-11-16 it failed in the six
modes that take an image, a clip or audio; it is now wired for all eight modes that offer it,
which is verified on a tiny test checkpoint. On the Space after the fixes (2026-11-16) it ran
at 704×480 with 49 frames in text to video with sound and in audio to video, and returned
1408×960: in the first with added detail at native size and not washed out, in the second a
coherent picture in colour, at peaks of 55.91 and 56.54 GiB reserved. On 2026-11-17 it
returned 1408×960 at 5 and 10 s and 1920×1088 at 10 s, the last from 960×544 in a 154 s GPU
window (§10.3 of [`docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md`](docs/SPACE_MODE_MATRIX.md)); a larger request is
refused on the Space before any GPU time
([the limits](#limits-the-app-checks-before-it-reserves-a-gpu)).

## The page

The whole design is in [`docs/UI_DESIGN.md`](docs/UI_DESIGN.md). A few things it does that are
easy to miss:

- **The line beside Generate** names what the run returns: its size, with "upscaled" when
  Upscale ×2 is on, or "Sound" in a sound-only mode, then the length and, on the Space, the quota
  it needs ("1408 × 960 upscaled, 5.0 s, needs 210 s quota"); elsewhere it says where the run
  happens instead.
- **The monitor keeps your run** when you pick another mode. While it does, and that mode has a
  sample, the button under the purpose line reads "See an example of" and the mode's name, and
  puts that sample on the monitor; then the same button reads "Back to your run" and puts your
  run back.
- **The monitor's player works by keyboard and touch**, and so does a loaded clip's preview:
  the controls show on keyboard focus, while the video is paused and on a touch screen; Play is
  named "Play" or "Pause", and only Enter and Space act on it, so Tab moves on without
  starting or stopping the clip.
- **The Done card** says where the length came from: "chosen by the model" only when the
  model's length lies strictly inside the range you allowed, otherwise "the shortest you
  allowed" or "the longest you allowed" (the run's note then says what the model chose). A run
  of under half a second took "under 1 s". After a video with sound, "Download the sound (WAV)"
  beside Reuse seed gives the sound track on its own.
- **A rejected adapter** gets one next step for its kind, such as "Use an adapter made for
  LTX-2.5." or "Download the file again, then upload it."
- **Restore settings**, in Recent runs, says what it did until your next run: "Settings from
  08:06 restored; the seed is random again, as it was for that run. Your current audio stays as
  it is."

## Examples on the page

Every mode comes with at least one example, which fills the form and runs nothing, and a
sample output on the monitor. They live in `examples/`: `manifest.json` lists them, and the
inputs and the samples are stored with Git LFS. The inputs were made for this project's tests,
and every sample is a re-encode of this app's own output from a real-weights run on a Mac;
[`examples/PROVENANCE.md`](examples/PROVENANCE.md) says where each file comes from. A clone
without Git LFS has small pointer files in their place: the page then leaves out every example
and sample that needs one, keeps the examples that are only a prompt, and shows its designed
idle monitor instead of a sample.

## Known issues

- **A warning in the log on page loads.** Gradio 6.24.0 logs `RuntimeWarning: coroutine
  'App.create_app.<locals>.get_current_user' was never awaited` when a page loads. It comes
  from Gradio's own route, not from the Studio, and it is fixed upstream in Gradio 6.25.0. The
  Studio stays on 6.24.0 for now: Gradio is pinned in three places (`requirements.txt`,
  `pyproject.toml` and the Space's `sdk_version`), and a bump touches the whole UI, so it gets
  a change of its own.

  superseded since.

The governing rule behind all of them, and behind this app's noisier error messages: the
predecessor's worst outage was not a model failure. A generation failed, four layers each
turned that failure into a falsy value, and the UI rendered an empty path as what looked
like a form quietly resetting itself. No error, no log, no signal. **It hid for a week.**

So: an empty output is an error at the source, every layer propagates it, there is no
"newest file in the output directory" fallback, each run gets its own output directory, and
there is no code path in which you press Generate, wait, and see nothing change. That includes
a result the page itself cannot show: until 2026-11-16 a file Gradio refused to serve left the
page on "Generating…" for good; now such a run ends with Generate back and a card that says the
run finished but its result could not be shown, with the server's own message.
